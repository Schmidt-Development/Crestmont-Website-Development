# Crestmont Frontend Research: Reference Sites

Research date: 2026-10-27. Purpose: inform the redesign of the Crestmont Website Development marketing site, client portal and owner/admin dashboard. The brand is black, gold and white with a gold crest logo.

## Method and limits

- I used WebSearch and design galleries only to find candidates. Each site below was then fetched directly with WebFetch, which confirmed it returned content on the research date.
- WebFetch returns extracted page text and structure, not raw CSS or JS. Direct `curl` downloads were blocked by the network proxy. So **motion claims are mostly inferred** from markup clues: duplicated rows that suggest marquees, menu close controls, video elements, "scroll" prompts and staged UI text. None of these sites exposed `IntersectionObserver`, `startViewTransition` or `prefers-reduced-motion` in the fetched text. Check the live pages with DevTools before relying on any motion detail.
- **Observed** means it was present in the fetched content. **Inference** is marked *(inferred)*.
- I make no claims about price, revenue or conversion performance. All wording is paraphrased.
- Excluded: koto.studio (robots blocked the fetch) and vercel.com/dashboard (needs auth; the Vercel docs were used instead). ueno.co was fetched but dropped because its homepage shows no work.

## Synthesis: principles to apply

1. **One gold accent per viewport.** Gold marks the single most important thing on screen: the primary CTA, the active nav item or the current project stage. Everything else is black, white and neutral greys. (Designjoy, thoughtbot and Vercel each lead with one primary CTA.)
2. **One contact verb, repeated in three places.** Choose one label (for example "Start a project") and use it in the header, hero and closing band. Every other link is a secondary text link. (thoughtbot, Locomotive, Upstatement)
3. **Restrained hero: one sentence, one proof point, one CTA.** No carousel. Put proof (a featured project or one short client quote) directly below. (Work & Co, thoughtbot)
4. **Services framed by client situation, linked to filtered work.** Each service card ends in a link to the portfolio pre-filtered to that service. (thoughtbot, basement.studio)
5. **Numbered process with progressive disclosure.** Show three to five numbered steps (Discover → Design → Build → Launch → Care). Each opens or links to detail. Reuse the same stage names in the portal tracker. (Sparkbox, Designjoy, Basecamp)
6. **Two-tier portfolio.** Large image cards for three to six flagship projects, then a compact text list, with filter chips and a closing "See all" card. (14islands, Instrument, Locomotive, Pentagram)
7. **Editorial section intros.** Every major section opens with a short eyebrow, an H2 and one or two sentences before its grid. Section rhythm alternates text-led and visual-led blocks, divided by thin rules. (Pentagram, Family, Sparkbox)
8. **Motion that explains or confirms, never gates.** Use reveal-on-enter for section intros, staged steps in process mockups, and toasts, drawers and "Copied" states in the portal. Heavy media (a reel, a crest animation) is opt-in with play and pause controls. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. (Lusion, Raycast, Emil Kowalski, rauno.me)
9. **FAQ accordion and inline short form before the footer.** Handle objections, then a five-field project form (name, email, company, project type, message). (Designjoy, Upstatement)
10. **Portal: left rail + page header with one primary action.** Rail: Overview, Projects, Invoices, Files, Support. Each page header has the title, a status summary and one gold primary button (for example "Pay invoice" or "New request"). (Stripe, Vercel)
11. **Attention queue first on dashboard home.** Admin and client Home both start with "needs your attention" items (overdue invoices, pending approvals, open tickets, readiness blockers). KPI widgets come after. (Stripe Home)
12. **One ordered status set per object, used everywhere.** Statuses group lists, become board columns and drive pill colours. Admin can toggle list or board, choose visible properties and save views. (Linear)
13. **CRM pipeline cards carry decision data.** Each card shows amount, next step, owner and days in stage. Column headers carry stage totals. Clicking a record opens a side panel with fields plus an activity timeline. (Attio)
14. **Keyboard and scope affordances for power users.** Cmd+K command menu, a "?" shortcut sheet, and a client scope switcher in admin. Nav items show counts where useful, such as "Support 3". (Vercel, Stripe, basement.studio)

## Anti-patterns to avoid

1. **Scroll-hijacking or forced scroll toll.** Content should never sit behind pinned, scrubbed scroll sequences or repeated "keep scrolling" prompts. Native scroll stays native.
2. **Gold everywhere.** Gold borders, icons, headings and buttons all at once flatten the hierarchy and look cheap. Treat gold as scarce.
3. **Marquee overload.** Several duplicated, auto-scrolling logo, tag and feature rows on one page make it noisy and hard to read, and they distract people with vestibular sensitivity. Use one static proof strip at most.
4. **Dashboard of charts with no actions.** KPI tiles above the fold that don't link anywhere bury the overdue invoice or open ticket that needs attention.
5. **Siloed portal objects.** Files, messages and invoices that live only in separate top-level sections with no link back to their project force clients to search across tabs. Every object should show its parent project.
